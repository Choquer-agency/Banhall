# Graded Foam Filter Program: Trial Log Summary, Fiscal 2026

Prepared by Ines Castellanos, Process Engineering, Brackenridge Kilnworks Ltd., Dunmore Falls, Ontario. Covers July 1 2025 to June 30 2026. Reviewed by Rolf Aberline, VP Engineering, on July 14 2026.

## Purpose

The program set out to make a replicated alumina foam filter with a pore-size gradient through its thickness for aluminium foundries. Two goals were carried together:

- Reduce filter breakage and thermal shock cracking during the pour. Fiscal 2025 field returns from two major accounts showed 3.8 percent of used 50 mm filters with visible cracking.
- Raise capture of fine oxide inclusions (20 to 80 micron) for thin-wall castings, without dropping flow rate more than 10 percent below the standard 20 ppi filter.

Program start: August 18 2025. Dedicated technician: Pavel Dunstan (full time from September 2 2025). Process lead: Ines Castellanos (about 50 percent of time).

## Working hypothesis

If a two-zone template (10 ppi entry, 30 ppi exit) is coated so both zones carry similar slurry mass per unit volume, linear firing shrinkage across the interface will match within 0.5 percent and the part will survive firing without delamination. If it survives, fine inclusion capture should rise at least 30 percent over the 20 ppi standard with no more than a 10 percent flow penalty.

## Trial summary

**Trial 1 (September 8 to 26 2025).** Adhesive-bonded 10/30 ppi sponge sheets, 25 mm each. Single dip, alumina slurry at 2,400 mPa.s. Standard firing, 1,580 C peak, 2 hour hold. 24 parts made, 18 delaminated (75 percent). Shrinkage mismatch on survivors: 1.4 percent. Slurry pick-up measured at 0.31 g/cm3 on the 30 ppi zone against 0.19 g/cm3 on the 10 ppi zone. Adhesive left a visible burnout gap of 0.3 to 0.6 mm.

**Trial 2 (October 6 to November 21 2025).** Thermal-fused templates. Two-stage dip: full part in 1,500 mPa.s slurry, coarse half only in 2,400 mPa.s slurry. Pick-up now 0.24 g/cm3 fine against 0.22 g/cm3 coarse. 40 parts, 9 delaminated (22 percent). Mismatch 0.7 percent. Ten survivors retained for later shock comparison.

**Trial 3 (December 1 2025 to January 23 2026).** Two-stage dip kept. Firing changed: 1 C/min ramp from 1,100 to 1,350 C, 45 minute hold at 1,350 C, then standard ramp to 1,580 C. 36 parts, 2 delaminated (6 percent). Mismatch 0.4 percent, within target. Added kiln time: about 3 hours per load (from 19 to 22 hours door-to-door).

**Trial 4 (February 2 to 27 2026).** Flow and capture rig. 50 kg A356 per pour at 720 C. Six pours each of 20 ppi, 30 ppi and graded (25 mm fine zone).

| Filter | Flow (kg/s) | Priming head vs 20 ppi | Fine inclusion capture vs 20 ppi |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 ppi standard | 4.1 | baseline | baseline |
| 30 ppi standard | 2.9 | +30 percent | +58 percent |
| Graded, 25 mm fine | 3.8 | +8 percent | +41 percent |

Choking observed late in 2 of 6 graded pours (flow fell below 2.5 kg/s after about 38 kg).

**Trial 5 (March 3 to 27 2026).** Graded filter with 15 mm fine zone and 35 mm coarse zone. Eight pours. Flow 3.9 kg/s held across full 50 kg, no choking. Capture +36 percent over 20 ppi.

**Trial 6 (April 6 to 30 2026).** Thermal shock. Quench from 700 C into water at 21 C, then crush test. Retained strength: 20 ppi standard 55 percent, graded (trial 3 method) 68 percent. Cold burst test, 300 mm metal head, 20 parts each: 3 of 20 standard cracked, 1 of 20 graded cracked with the crack stopping at the interface. Trial 2 retained parts (0.7 percent mismatch): 4 of 10 cracked along the interface.

## Findings by uncertainty

- **Firing survival.** Shrinkage mismatch tracks slurry mass per volume more than pore size. Two-stage dip plus slow ramp through 1,100 to 1,350 C brings mismatch under 0.5 percent. Adhesive bonding is not viable.
- **Capture without choking.** A 15 mm fine exit zone gives about a 36 percent capture gain with a flow penalty near 5 percent. A 25 mm fine zone captures more but chokes late in the pour.
- **Thermal shock.** A well-matched interface raises retained strength and arrests cracks. A poorly matched interface becomes the failure plane.

## Field trial

Customer pour trial, May 11 to June 19 2026, one foundry, A356 thin-wall housings. 200 graded filters used, zero breakage. X-ray reject rate on housings moved from 6.2 percent to 4.5 percent over the same period.

## Open items for fiscal 2027

- Grading at 30 mm total thickness (highest volume format).
- Continuous gradient rather than two zones.
- Binder change to shorten the slow ramp; target added kiln time under 1 hour.
- Second foundry and a second alloy (A380) before any launch decision.
