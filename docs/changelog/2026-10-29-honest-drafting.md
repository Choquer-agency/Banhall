# Drafts stick to what the sources say, and ask each question once

Drafts now follow how the firm's best approved reports are written: they report what the sources show, say plainly what is still open, and stop filling a fixed story with things nobody said.

### Improved
- An experiment is written as far as the sources go: what was tried, what happened, what changed and what was learned or left open. Where a step is missing, the paragraph ends with one gap note that names everything missing, instead of a made-up result or a gap for every sentence.
- A hypothesis states the outcome expected and why, or the knowledge sought. It carries a number only when the sources give one.
- Line 242 names the standard approach a practitioner would use, what it does and why it falls short here, instead of saying "no documented methods existed". Where the sources do not name one, the draft may name common practice with an inference note for you to confirm with the client.
- Line 246 gives each uncertainty its status as the sources show it (resolved, resolved for the conditions tested, partially resolved or unresolved), and never writes a goal as if it had been achieved. Where nothing has been learned yet about an uncertainty, the draft says where the work stands, with one gap note, instead of a made-up finding.
- The QA score now checks the draft against your interviews and files, not only a summary of them, and never takes points off for an honest gap.
- A question for the client is asked once: a later Line no longer repeats a gap an earlier Line already asks.
- A draft may run up to 20 percent over a Line's CRA word and line limits when it needs the room, instead of being squeezed while it is written. Trim it to fit before you export: the Line's meter, the export window and the Assistant's shorten options show where. A Line shortened to fit while it is written keeps the length you chose.
- The Assistant no longer shortens a Line you did not ask about because it is over a limit while you draft. It offers a shorter version only when you asked about length, or when its edit would take the Line more than 20 percent over.
- The project questionnaire asks for the usual way to solve the problem and why it did not work, and for what the team expected and why, instead of a measurable outcome.
