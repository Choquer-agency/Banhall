# Adversarial Review — PRD: Banhall PD generation — better than the dump

Reviewer stance: skeptical senior engineer + skeptical SR&ED writer (Larry) + skeptical CRA reviewer. Targets: `prd.md`, `addendum.md`. Evidence base: `extract-codebase.md`, `extract-web-research.md`, `extract-competitor-mechanisms.md`.

## Verdict

The PRD is unusually well-grounded — every mechanism claim is tied to a code line or a cited source, and the glossary/FR discipline is real — but its two load-bearing claims are not actually secured: the Generation Brief has no mechanism to catch a wrong Storyline once sections are checked against it (it can make drafts *more confidently wrong*, not just better), and the Paired Comparison that is supposed to prove "better than ChatGPT" is structurally unblind, self-graded in part by the tool under test, and run on n=4 with the dev/tune project (25001) doubling as an eval project. Ship the Brief and profile-fidelity work; do not let SM-1/SM-3 stand as written as the September proof point without the fixes below.

## Findings

### Critical

**1. The Storyline can lock in an error with no way back**
§3 Storyline / §4.1 FR-1 — "a section that contradicts it fails the Self-check" (FR-1); Storyline is defined as "the controlling narrative every section of the PD must follow." FR-1 derives one Storyline *before any section is drafted* and every later section is checked *against* it, but nothing checks the Storyline against what the sections turn up. There is no backward-validation path — only forward propagation (Storyline → sections) and manual Brief-edit-and-regenerate (FR-3/FR-4). If Brief derivation gets the controlling uncertainty wrong (a common SR&ED failure mode — conflating business risk with technological uncertainty), every section is then *mechanically forced toward* that wrong narrative, and a section that happens to get it right will fail the Self-check and be "repaired" back into line. This is the opposite of quality improvement in that failure case, and it is the single biggest way the PRD's own thesis (structuring beats dumping) could invert.
**Fix:** add an FR (or a Self-check criterion) that treats Storyline-section contradiction as a signal to re-examine the Storyline, not only the section; require the Brief to show its own confidence/derivation basis, not just its output; add a counter-metric tracking how often Storyline edits happen *after* a section reveals a contradiction.

**2. Confidence Map hedging is writer-facing and optional; the CRA-facing draft may not inherit it**
§3 Confidence Map ("facts classified as established, partially established, unresolved, or unreliable... tied to its source") / §4.1 FR-2 / Cross-Cutting Provenance NFR. The Confidence Map is shown "beside the draft" to the writer (§4.1) and is explicitly optional to read (FR-3). Nothing in FR-2, FR-9 or FR-10 requires that an "unresolved" or "unreliable" fact retain hedged language *inside the drafted PD section* — the vision explicitly wants "one coherent storyline," which is in tension with preserving uncertainty in the prose a CRA reviewer actually reads. `extract-competitor-mechanisms.md` quotes an ex-HMRC inspector on exactly this pattern in a comparable tool: AI "might sanitise a narrative by hiding weaknesses." The PRD names this research finding in the addendum but never turns it into an FR constraint on the draft's *output* language — only on the writer's *sidebar* view.
**Fix:** add a testable consequence to FR-9 or FR-10: a section describing a Confidence-Map "unresolved"/"unreliable" fact must carry CRA-appropriate hedging in the drafted prose itself, not just in the Brief; add this to the self-check criteria list explicitly (it currently checks Storyline/Exclusions/Glossary/paragraph rules/Locked Rules — not confidence calibration).

**3. The Paired Comparison is not blind, is partly self-graded by the tool under test, and reuses the dev/tune project as an eval project**
§4.5 FR-16, §7 SM-1, addendum D. Three compounding problems:
- **Blinding:** addendum D says drafts are "labelled A/B, order randomised" — but Banhall's output carries a distinctive skeleton (242/244/246 headings, one glossary, Compliance Notes referencing the writer's own rules back to them). A judge who wrote the settings will recognize which draft is "speaking their language" within a paragraph, defeating blinding and introducing a halo effect (the mere presence of "your instruction was applied up to the cap" is a demand-characteristic cue, not evidence of quality).
- **Self-grading asymmetry:** addendum D: "Deviations = items in a Deviation Inventory against the writer's settings, counted by the writer (the tool's inventory is a starting list, not the count)." FR-12's Deviation Inventory is a Banhall-only feature — the Banhall draft's deviation count starts from a list the system generated about itself, while the ChatGPT draft's deviation count must be found manually with no tool support. The system being measured seeds its own evidence.
- **n=4, single judge, overfit set:** SM-1 requires "≥ 3 of 4" projects and 25001 is both the PRD's running example/gold standard (referenced throughout UJ-1, addendum G) *and* one of only four projects used to validate SM-1 — the same project the Brief/self-check machinery will inevitably be tuned against during build. Judge = Larry, who is under explicit organizational pressure ("adoption is the test case," "one bad week from giving up") in both directions.
Open Question 3 ("Is 'Sol' via OpenRouter the same model behaviour as ChatGPT's Sol?") is also left unresolved and is not gated before FR-16/SM-1 run — if the baseline model differs materially from the real ChatGPT product (different system prompt, no canvas, different tool access), "better than ChatGPT" is not what got measured.
**Fix:** strip Banhall-specific chrome (headings, Compliance Notes, glossary callouts) before blind rating, or have a third party format both drafts identically; have deviation counting done by the same method for both drafts (manual, by the writer, without FR-12 assistance) or disclose the asymmetry in the recorded comparison; add at least one project untouched during Brief/self-check development to the eval set; resolve OQ-3 before running SM-1, or record the model-equivalence caveat directly on every comparison record per FR-16's own schema.

**4. "Zero writer-authored artifacts" (SM-3) can be satisfied while Larry does the same authoring work, just relabeled as editing**
§7 SM-3 ("no writer produces a storyline, exclusion list, confidence map or glossary offline for a Banhall project after launch") vs §4.1 FR-3 ("An edited Brief entry is used verbatim by the next generation and recorded as writer-edited"). SM-3's test is "offline" vs. "in-tool" — it says nothing about *how much* editing happens inside the Brief. If Storyline derivation is weak (Finding 1), Larry's real workaround is to substantially rewrite the Storyline/exclusion list/glossary inside the Brief editor every generation — which is writer-authoring the same four artifacts the PRD exists to eliminate, just moved from a separate document into an in-tool form. SM-3 would read as a pass throughout. No SM or counter-metric measures Brief-edit volume, frequency, or time spent — SM-C4 only measures the *option* to skip the Brief (time-to-first-draft with no interaction), not the cost when a writer must engage with it.
**Fix:** add a counter-metric on Brief-edit magnitude (e.g., % of generations with a substantially rewritten Storyline, or edit-distance on Brief entries) so the effort-ceiling promise is checked at the level Larry will actually feel it.

### High

**5. FR-5's "default for every mode" is ambiguous about whether it imports the human-review gate, and either answer is bad**
§4.2 FR-5, addendum B. Code fact: "`iterative` already runs 242 → 244 → 246 with human review and approved-prior-section context... `single/compare` run the three sections in parallel." FR-5 says the system will "feed each generated section into the next" by default, tagged `[ASSUMPTION: ordered generation with prior-section context becomes the default for every mode.]` — but it never says whether single/compare modes also gain the human-approval gate between sections that the cited code mechanism ("approved-prior-section-context") depends on. Two readings, both costly: (a) gated — every generation now requires a human checkpoint between 242 and 244 by default, which is a workflow change nobody asked for and directly threatens SM-C4 (writer interruptions must not grow); (b) ungated — 244 is written against a 242 draft the writer hasn't reviewed or approved, so an error in 242 propagates straight into 244 and 246 *before the writer ever sees anything*, which is strictly worse than today's parallel generation (where a bad 242 doesn't poison 244).
**Fix:** FR-5 must specify explicitly which behavior ships, and if ungated, note that "approved-prior-section-context" is not the mechanism being reused — a different, weaker guarantee is.

**6. FR-17's title oversells; the real ceiling doesn't move**
§4.6 FR-17 ("Document ceiling matches the writer's working set") vs §6.2 ("Raising the hard document cap to 40 in context (only the visibility part ships)"). Code fact (`extract-codebase.md` §4): "Effective cap: 12 documents into chat evidence... and analyzer/generation context." FR-17's consequence — "Attaching 40 documents succeeds; the Brief lists which entered context" — is true only because "attach" and "enter context" are different things; the FR title claims parity with ChatGPT's working set while MVP explicitly keeps the 12-document context cap and ships only better disclosure of what got cut. For Larry, whose original complaint pattern is "my inputs were silently overridden/discarded" (PSOS-49/50), being told what was cut is real progress, but the FR framing risks re-raising the same trust issue in a new form if he reads "document ceiling matches working set" as "all 40 get read."
**Fix:** rename/reframe FR-17 around disclosure, not capacity ("Context exclusions are visible" rather than "ceiling matches working set"); make the attach/context-inclusion distinction explicit in the FR text, not only in the non-goals appendix.

**7. The 2x latency/cost NFR doesn't count FR-9's repair pass**
Cross-Cutting NFRs: "the Brief and Self-checks add at most one model call per section plus one for the Brief; end-to-end generation time and cost stay within 2x today's single-mode run" `[ASSUMPTION: confirm]`. FR-9: "A section containing an excluded claim, an off-glossary synonym, or a cap breach is repaired **or flagged** before display" and FR-5: "A Self-check that fails triggers a repair attempt." A repair attempt is an additional model call beyond the "one call per section" the NFR budgets — for a 3-section PD with a plausible per-section repair rate (checking against five separate criteria: Storyline, Claim Exclusions, Glossary, paragraph rules, Locked Rules), the "at most one" ceiling is arithmetically wrong on its own terms whenever any check fails, which for a 5-criteria check across 3 sections is not a rare case.
**Fix:** either budget repair calls explicitly in the NFR or cap the number of repair attempts counted toward the 2x target and report overruns as a distinct signal, not silently absorbed.

**8. "No excluded claim appears" / "synonym check finds none" are asserted as guarantees the cited evidence doesn't support**
§4.1 FR-2: "A Claim Exclusion never appears in the draft; a test corpus with a prominent excluded claim yields a draft without it" and "a synonym check over the draft finds none." No synonym-detection tooling is cited anywhere in `extract-codebase.md`, and the addendum's own cited research on self-critique — "Self-Refine (arxiv 2303.17651): generate → self-critique → revise, ~20% avg gain" — describes a partial average improvement, not elimination. An LLM self-check catching every paraphrase of a prominent, salient excluded claim, or every off-glossary synonym, is exactly the kind of semantic-detection task the cited research says is unreliable at 100%. The FR states these as binary pass/fail testable consequences with no tolerance or sampling methodology.
**Fix:** reframe as a rate target with a defined test methodology (adversarial paraphrase corpus, sampled), not an absolute; name the detection mechanism (rule-based glossary matcher vs. LLM judgment) since they have very different reliability profiles.

**9. FR-12/13's Deviation Inventory only covers Writer-Profile rule violations, not content/factual corrections**
§3 Deviation ("a place where the draft departs from the Writer Profile"), §4.4 FR-12 ("the assistant lists every paragraph, its corresponding Writer Profile rule, and each Deviation"). UJ-1's climax is partly about content ("the results discussion" needing expansion up to a cap), but a large share of Larry's real corrections in practice are likely to be factual/content judgments that don't map to an enumerable Profile rule (a settings document cannot pre-specify every future judgment call). Anything outside the rule set is, by FR-12's own definition, not inventoriable and therefore not covered by FR-13's "one coordinated pass." For that class of correction, Larry is still in a multi-turn chat negotiation — the exact failure mode (partial completion, ~39% multi-turn adherence drop per the cited Microsoft research) the PRD names as the problem it's solving.
**Fix:** either extend Deviation to include writer-flagged content issues (not just rule violations) so FR-13 can coordinate them too, or explicitly scope FR-12/13 as "style/rule convergence only" and name the residual content-correction path in the PRD rather than implying full convergence.

**10. FR-15 (Reference PD comparison) — the PRD's own author flags it as "emotionally load-bearing for Larry" and defers it anyway**
§6.2: "FR-15 Reference PD comparison as an in-product feature — v1.1; the Paired Comparison uses it manually first. `[NOTE FOR PM: emotionally load-bearing for Larry; pull in if FR-13 lands early.]`" This is the mechanism closest to what Larry's self-graded 70→80 iteration process actually was (comparing against a known-good standard). Deferring it to v1.1 means that post-launch, on the exact workflow the PRD was written to fix, Larry has no in-tool way to ask "how does this compare to what I know is right" — the most likely residual ChatGPT/manual-diff behavior of all, and it directly threatens SM-5 (ChatGPT usage drop) without any offsetting note in that metric's definition.
**Fix:** at minimum, note the SM-5 interpretation risk this creates, or de-scope something smaller (e.g., part of FR-17's visibility work) to pull FR-15 into MVP given the PM's own note.

**11. Stored Claim Exclusions / Confidence Map create an un-addressed discoverability risk**
Cross-Cutting NFRs — Provenance: "every Brief entry, Compliance Note and Completion Report line is stored with the generation and linked to source passages; nothing in the draft is unexplained." §3 Claim Exclusions: "statements from the Dump that must not appear in the PD however prominent they are in the source." Taken together, the system will maintain an indefinitely stored, per-project record explicitly enumerating facts the writer/system chose to leave out of a CRA submission "however prominent" they were in the interview. Under the CRA's Pre-Claim Approval process (effective 2026-04-01, cited in §"Why Now"), if underlying documentation is ever requested, a stored artifact that reads as "here is what we knowingly excluded" is a materially different risk posture than simply not having generated such a list. The PRD's Provenance NFR treats storing everything as unconditionally good; it isn't examined for this specific downside.
**Fix:** get a legal/compliance read on retention and framing of Claim Exclusions before shipping the NFR as written; consider whether exclusion rationale needs to be scoped to eligibility (not "reads badly") — see Finding 17.

### Medium

**12. FR-1 cites the wrong FR for its own Self-check mechanism**
§4.1 FR-1: "a section that contradicts it fails the Self-check (FR-11)." FR-11 is "Context completeness is visible," not the Self-check FR — that's FR-9 ("Self-check before output"). A small thing, but in a PRD whose main claimed strength is rigor (every mechanism cited to a line number), a self-referential numbering error in the very first FR is the kind of thing a skeptical engineer catches in the first pass and uses to discount the rest of the document's internal consistency.
**Fix:** correct the cross-reference; add a numbering/cross-reference lint pass before this ships to implementers.

**13. "Sol" is never defined in the Glossary despite being central to the one metric meant to prove the whole PRD**
§3 (no "Sol" entry) vs its use throughout UJ-1 ("(2) picks Sol"), UJ-4 ("ChatGPT (Sol)... into Banhall"), FR-16, and Open Question 3 ("Is 'Sol' via OpenRouter the same model behaviour as ChatGPT's Sol?"). The Glossary defines PD, Transcript, Dump, and every other load-bearing noun precisely — but not the one term whose ambiguity the PRD itself flags as an open risk to its primary success metric. This isn't just a documentation gap; it's evidence the equivalence question hasn't actually been resolved even at the definitional level.
**Fix:** add a Glossary entry for "Sol" that states, precisely, which product/model is meant in each context (Banhall's OpenRouter-routed `openai/gpt-5.6-sol` vs. whatever Larry uses at chatgpt.com), and resolve OQ-3 before it's used as a glossary-level assumption.

**14. FR-1 has no citation/provenance testable consequence, unlike FR-2**
§4.1 FR-1 vs FR-2. FR-2 requires: "Every Confidence Map entry cites a Transcript or Supporting Document passage." FR-1 (Storyline) has no equivalent testable consequence requiring the Storyline to be traceable to source passages, even though the Storyline is the single most load-bearing Brief artifact (it gates every section via Self-check, per Finding 1). The Cross-Cutting Provenance NFR asserts this generally ("every Brief entry... linked to source passages") but FR-1 itself doesn't operationalize it the way FR-2 does for its three artifacts.
**Fix:** add a Storyline citation requirement to FR-1's consequences, matching FR-2's rigor.

**15. MVP scope is nearly the entire FR set — "MVP" in name only**
§6.1: "FR-1 to FR-4 (Generation Brief), FR-5 to FR-8 (profile fidelity), FR-9 to FR-11 (draft quality), FR-12 to FR-14 (one-pass convergence), FR-16 (Paired Comparison harness), FR-17 at the level of 'tell the writer what was included.'" That's 16 of 17 FRs — Brief derivation, ordered-generation default change, five-criteria self-check with repair, Compliance Notes, Deviation Inventory, Coordinated Revision with structured per-item reporting, and the comparison harness, all as one release. No phased cut is considered (e.g., ship Brief + Self-check first, defer Deviation Inventory/Coordinated Revision), and no build-risk or sequencing note appears in Open Questions despite the breadth.
**Fix:** if this really is one release, say so and drop "MVP" framing, or propose an actual phase boundary within 4.1–4.4 rather than treating "MVP" as "everything except FR-15 and multi-select."

**16. No counter-metric for self-check over-triggering (false positives)**
§7 SM-C3: "QA gate failures and fabrication flags... must not increase; the Brief must not introduce facts." This watches for the system adding wrong content, not for the self-check/repair loop incorrectly flagging or rewriting *correct* content (e.g., a true statement that superficially resembles an excluded claim, or a valid synonym misflagged as off-glossary). An over-aggressive gate is a distinct failure mode from fabrication and isn't covered by any listed SM or counter-metric.
**Fix:** add a counter-metric sampling repaired sections for whether the repair was necessary (a spot-check against the pre-repair version), not just whether QA/fabrication flags rose.

### Low

**17. Claim Exclusion rationale isn't constrained to eligibility, leaving room for "looks bad, hide it"**
§3: "Claim Exclusions — statements from the Dump that must not appear in the PD however prominent they are in the source." The definition doesn't distinguish "ineligible under CRA's five questions" (legitimate scoping) from "prominent but unflattering" (selective omission). Given the CRA-defensibility framing of the whole PRD, this ambiguity should be closed explicitly, not left to derivation-time judgment with no stated criterion.
**Fix:** define Claim Exclusions as eligibility-scoped only, and require the Brief to state the exclusion reason category alongside each entry (already close to what Compliance Notes do elsewhere in the PRD — extend the pattern here).

**18. FR-6's third consequence is a documentation task, not a generation-behavior test**
§4.2 FR-6: "The precedence is stated in `docs/product-domain.md` as an amendment." This is a fine requirement but doesn't belong in the same bulleted list as behavioral test assertions ("A profile instruction contradicting each of the six House Rule categories is applied in the draft") — it will pass or fail independent of whether the system actually behaves correctly, which weakens the "testable consequences" contract the PRD otherwise holds to consistently.
**Fix:** move doc-update requirements to a separate checklist, not the FR's testable-consequences list.

**19. SM-5 can't distinguish "tool isn't better" from expected residual ChatGPT use**
§7 SM-5 ("writers report using ChatGPT for PD drafting on fewer projects each week... they may keep it for other work") vs §5 Non-Goals ("CAD/DWG ingestion... ~10% of PDs"). CAD-heavy projects are an explicit, acknowledged carve-out where ChatGPT use should rationally continue post-launch, plus FR-15's deferral (Finding 10) creates a second expected carve-out. SM-5 as written has no attribution mechanism to separate these known-expected causes from a genuine adoption failure signal, making the metric hard to interpret honestly in either direction.
**Fix:** when reporting SM-5 weekly, tag each remaining ChatGPT use with a reason (CAD, reference-comparison, other/unclear) so the metric isn't just a number Michael has to take on faith.

## What survives

- **The precedence rule is not invented — it's grounded and merely extended.** FR-6's Locked > Writer Profile > House ordering is already in `docs/product-domain.md:1358-1360`; the PRD adds reporting on top of an existing, working rule rather than proposing new governance from scratch. Hard to attack because it isn't new policy, just visibility.
- **FR-11 (context completeness visible) closes a real, previously-identified, still-open gap.** Sept 8 intake finding 3 ("Incomplete context... cut notices not user-facing") was explicitly left unaddressed by PR #8 per `extract-codebase.md` §7. FR-11 is a direct, low-risk, easily-testable fix for a documented and still-live problem — not a speculative feature.
- **The Non-Goals list shows real scope discipline for a feature-heavy PRD.** Explicitly excluding ChatGPT embedding, CAD/DWG, Locked Rule changes, and "optimising an LLM self-score" (§5) heads off several obvious scope-creep and metric-gaming vectors before they start — SM-C1 in particular is a genuinely well-reasoned counter-metric grounded in cited research on LLM-judge length/grade-inflation bias.
- **The counter-metrics (SM-C1–C4) show the PRD authors already anticipated several of this review's attack angles** — length/self-score gaming, latency/cost blowout, fabrication-flag increases, and writer-interruption creep are all named, even if (per Findings 7, 16) the arithmetic and coverage aren't fully airtight yet. That's a materially better starting position than a PRD with no counter-metrics at all.
- **Every mechanism claim is traceable to a specific code line or cited source** (`addendum.md` §B, with file:line citations for every code fact). This is unusual rigor that makes the PRD's claims falsifiable rather than aspirational — which is exactly why the gaps found above (FR-1/FR-11 mismatch, FR-17's title vs. actual cap, FR-5's gating ambiguity) are catchable at all: the PRD gives you enough to check its own work.
- **Open Questions §8 is honest, not performative.** It surfaces the 246-cap-vs-house-rule ambiguity, the mode-discovery question, the Sol/ChatGPT equivalence risk (OQ-3), and judge-sufficiency (OQ-4) as genuinely unresolved — the PRD doesn't pretend these are settled, even though (Findings 3, 13) they should have gated the metrics that depend on them rather than being left open alongside them.
