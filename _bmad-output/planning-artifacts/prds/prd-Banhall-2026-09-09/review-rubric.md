# PRD Quality Review — Banhall PD generation — better than the dump

## Overall verdict

This PRD holds up: the thesis is stated and enforced ("the Dump is the maximum required input" — Constraints and Guardrails, prd.md:246), FRs carry testable, bounded consequences almost throughout, and the brownfield code anchors in `addendum.md` §B check out against the actual codebase (verified against `convex/ai/promptProgram.ts`, `convex/writerProfiles.ts`, `convex/ai/chatAgentV2.ts`, `convex/lib/lineLimits.ts`, `convex/lib/passageEdits.ts`, `docs/product-domain.md`, and others). What's at risk: an Open Question (§8 OQ6) that a scope decision two sections earlier (§6.2) has already answered, undermining trust in the Open Questions list as a genuine decision tracker; FR-6's precedence statement doesn't account for the org-mode tier that already exists in `convex/writerProfiles.ts:207-214` (`writer_choice|enforced|off`), which is a real brownfield gap, not a cosmetic one; and moderate case-drift on five Glossary terms in the narrative sections that a source-extracting downstream workflow would have to normalize.

## Decision-readiness — strong

The PRD names real trade-offs rather than smoothing them. `addendum.md` §A lays out four options (Dump-only, Writer-supplied pre-documents, System-derived Brief, Per-section multi-select) with an honest "why not" column grounded in evidence (Larry's Rev A→J being only "marginally better," Claimer being "criticised for shallow narratives"), and the PRD picks Option C while explicitly deferring Option D to v2 with a stated reason (§6.2: "it is C at paragraph granularity with choices, and because it adds interaction before the first draft (SM-C4)"). The NFR section states a real cost the reader must accept: "end-to-end generation time and cost stay within 2x today's single-mode run. `[ASSUMPTION: 2x is acceptable for the quality gain; confirm.]`" (prd.md:241) — a trade-off named, not hidden. `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts land at genuine tensions, not safe checkpoints: FR-15's deferral is flagged as "emotionally load-bearing for Larry; pull in if FR-13 lands early" (prd.md:265), which is a real pushback point acknowledged rather than dodged. A scan for red-flag smoothing language ("important," "balances," "values") found zero hits.

### Findings
- **high** Open Question 6 is already answered elsewhere in the document (§6.2, §8) — OQ6 asks "Document cap: is 12-in-context acceptable if the writer can see what was cut, or must the cap rise to 40 in MVP?" (prd.md:294). But §6.2 Out of Scope for MVP has already decided this: "Raising the hard document cap to 40 in context (only the visibility part ships)." (prd.md:267). A reader relying on §8 as the list of genuinely unresolved questions will not know this one is settled, and a downstream architecture pass could either re-litigate a closed decision or build to the wrong assumption if it reads OQ6 in isolation. *Fix:* either remove OQ6 (the decision is made) or rewrite it to ask the actually-open question (e.g., "when does the cap rise to 40, and what's the trigger?"), and cross-reference §6.2 from wherever it lands.
- **low** FR-6's precedence statement is proceeding ahead of its own dependency (Open Question 1). FR-6 states "Locked Rules > Writer Profile > House Rules" as settled precedence and specs concrete consequences (prd.md:147-152), while §4.2's own `[ASSUMPTION]` tag and OQ1 (prd.md:289) say whether the Section 246 cap is genuinely locked or a leaking House Rule is still undecided and "get it before Friday." The PRD does flag this honestly via the assumption tag, so this is not a smoothed-over gap — but a reader implementing FR-6 today is building against an unconfirmed premise. *Fix:* none required beyond what's there; consider adding "pending OQ1" to the FR-6 heading itself so the dependency is visible without cross-referencing.

## Substance over theater — strong

Three personas (Larry, Tracy, Michael) — under the four-persona ceiling — and each drives distinct FRs rather than existing for coverage: Larry drives FR-1–FR-14 across two UJs, Tracy specifically exercises the multi-transcript reconciliation path (FR-1's "three Transcripts... Storyline is one narrative" consequence, prd.md:115), and Michael drives FR-16 alone. No persona is decorative. The Vision (§1) is specific to this product — "drop in the interview transcripts... rely on the settings they wrote once" (prd.md:17) — and would not swap into another PRD unchanged. Cross-Cutting NFRs avoid boilerplate: "Latency and cost" gives a concrete 2x bound rather than "reasonable performance," and "Determinism where it matters" ties directly to FR-4 rather than asserting genericthe-system-must-be-reliable language. A scan of the full document for stock NFR/UX adjectives ("gracefully," "seamless," "robust," "user-friendly," "scalable") found none.

## Strategic coherence — strong

The thesis is explicit and load-bearing: "The difference is not more input from the writer. It is that Banhall does the structuring work the writer currently does by hand" (prd.md:19), operationalized as a hard constraint — "Any feature that requires the writer to author a new artifact violates this PRD" (prd.md:246) — that is then used to reject real candidate features (FR-15 and per-section multi-select are deferred specifically because they'd add writer interaction, not because they're hard). Feature grouping (4.1 Brief → 4.2 Profile fidelity → 4.3 Draft quality → 4.4 Convergence → 4.5 Proof → 4.6 Capacity) reads as an arc from derive-structure through prove-it-worked, not a capability list. Success Metrics validate the thesis directly rather than measuring activity: SM-1 (blind preference + deviation count), SM-2 (corrections-to-acceptable ≤ 1), SM-3 (zero writer-authored artifacts) all measure the stated problem, not usage volume. Counter-metrics are specific and paired to the SM they guard (SM-C1 guards against length/self-score gaming SM-1; SM-C4 guards against the Brief itself becoming the new writer burden it's meant to remove) — this is the strongest part of the document.
